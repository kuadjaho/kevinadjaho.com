Flagship project

e-Port.bj: the Port of Cotonou goes digital

The stakes

The Port of Cotonou, Benin's economic engine, needed to move from a set of siloed systems to a genuine digital ecosystem: a common backbone connecting customs, the single window, stevedores and terminals, secure and aligned with the sector's international standards (IMO, IPCSA). e-Port.bj Project Lead at the Autonomous Port of Cotonou (PAC) since Nov. 2024, I lead the implementation of the Port Information System Portal (PSIP): single sign-on (SSO), digital services, electronic document management and secure exchanges.

Governance

Within the programme's governance, I led or coordinated the drafting of the terms of reference, the integration specifications, the memorandum of understanding signed by the port community stakeholders, and the reform memoranda and decision papers taken up to the Presidency, in collaboration with the PAC's directorates. Day to day, I coordinate the technical and business teams of customs, terminals, banks, freight forwarders and state agencies, and I work with the experts of Port of Antwerp-Bruges International (POABI) on the governance and operating strategy of the portal.

Structuring choices

Single sign-on on WSO2 Identity Server; secure exchanges through the WSO2 API Gateway (OAuth2, national PKI); integrations with Customs (WEBB), GUCE.bj, terminal operators and banks; a business architecture designed upstream, during the procedure optimization work (2022 to 2024), so that the portal serves processes that are already harmonized.

Risks addressed

Regulatory compliance, followed with ASIN and APDP and aligned with the ISPS Code. The security of inter-agency exchanges, through authentication and encryption. Adoption, with a change management plan to anchor the platform in the institution. Delivery commitments, through contract follow-up and acceptance of deliverables.

Results

More than 15 import/export procedures digitalized for the port community. The Phase 1 and 2 deliverables (portal, document library, e-services platform) passed technical acceptance in late 2025 and were validated in early 2026. Internationally, I initiated Benin's request for IMO assistance on its Maritime Single Window, and served as the PAC focal point through to the IMO technical assistance mission at the Port of Cotonou (Dec. 2023), under the FAL Convention.

Operations

Keeping a port information system running, 24/7

Before designing platforms, I spent more than seven years running those of a container terminal that never stops, at APM Terminals (Maersk Group). That is where I learned what a system has to withstand.

24/7 availability

Administration of the Navis N4, XPS and ECN4 Terminal Operating Systems for round-the-clock operations: 99.8% uptime and level 1 and 2 technical support for the terminal's users.

Automated critical flows

Billing of more than 800,000 containers (TEU) a year, interfaced with the IFS ERP; EDI messaging (COREOR, CODECO, COARRI) to the EDIFACT standard and exchanges with the national Single Window (SEGUB): chains that must never stop.

Data, reporting and access

Administration of Microsoft SQL Server environments: operations, performance indicators, regulatory reporting, dashboards and access-control matrices for the terminal's users.

Field and equipment

Roll-out of vehicle-mounted terminals (VMT 8515, OMNII XT15) on trucks and stackers, DGPS geolocation of handling equipment, infrastructure services (DHCP, FTP, radio), and support to the TOS optimization of the Apapa terminal in Lagos.

That operations experience now guides the implementation of the PSIP at the Port of Cotonou: a system designed to be operated, monitored and maintained, not just delivered.
